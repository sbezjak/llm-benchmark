Judge reliability - position bias

Position bias is a known LLM-judge failure: the verdict follows which answer comes first, not which is better. How it is tested: show the grader the same two answers in both orders and check the verdict holds. llama3.2 (itself a contestant) flips on up to 45% of pairs, and 90% of those flips just follow whichever came first (paid grader: 14%) - order, not quality, decided it.

Self-preference

Self-preference bias (self-enhancement): a model-judge rating its own answers higher than a neutral judge does. How it is tested: let llama3.2 grade a pool that includes its own answers. The left column is how often each model wins under llama's judging; the right is its score from the independent paid grader. llama ranks itself third of 5 by its own judging, but the neutral grader puts it last - it favours itself. Limits

  • No best model. Too close, and the grader is biased.
  • Small n. 2 reps can't resolve a 0.003 gap.
  • One suite. 20 items - other questions could shift it.

What production would add

  • Grader outside the pool, or a panel of judges by majority.
  • More items, standard sets - hundreds to thousands, shared and public.
  • Extra reps only where scores are close.
  • Human spot-checks and drift monitoring in production.
